## THE ROOM

*Close your eyes.*

You push open a heavy wooden door. The hinges don't creak—they've been oiled by the same hands for thirty-five years.

**The light** is golden and slanted. Late afternoon pours through tall windows, catching dust motes that drift like meditation. No fluorescents. No harsh overhead. Just the honest light that leather workers have always known—the light that shows true color, reveals grain, forgives nothing.

**The smell** arrives before your eyes adjust: vegetable-tanned leather, warm and almost sweet. Beneath it, the clean bite of brass polish. A whisper of wood shavings from the lasts being shaped in the corner. Something older—felt, perhaps, or the ghosts of horsehair. The smell of work that takes time.

**The textures** are everywhere your hands want to go: the butter-soft curve of a finished boot waiting on the counter. The rough weave of a canvas apron hung on a peg. The cool smoothness of tools worn to perfect fit by decades of the same grip. The grain of the workbench, lighter where hands have rested.

**The sounds** are almost not there. The soft thump of a mallet on leather. The whisper of thread being pulled through. Occasional footsteps on wooden floors. No music. No chatter. Just the breathing rhythm of work being done properly.

**The temperature** is comfortable in the way old buildings are—not climate-controlled, but *right*. Warm from the bodies working, cool from the stone walls. The kind of temperature where you could stay all day and never think about it.

A man looks up from his work. He doesn't smile—he's in the middle of something—but his eyes are warm. He gestures to a chair. You sit. He returns to his work.

This is Domog.

*Now. Does your screen feel like that room?*

#### Justifications

**Warm (75%)** — Not intimate in a cloying way. Warm like a workshop, not like a hug. The warmth of hands that have done this work so long they've stopped counting.

**Minimalist-leaning (40%)** — Restrained, but not empty. Every element earns its place, but there *are* elements. Not the barrenness of tech minimalism—the considered simplicity of a craftsman's bench where only necessary tools remain.

**Traditional-leaning (65%)** — The techniques are centuries old. The values are permanent. But this isn't a museum—it's a living practice. Traditional in substance, contemporary in clarity.

**Natural (80%)** — Leather comes from animals. Thread from plants. Wood from trees. The steppe is horizon and earth. Nothing synthetic. Nothing fabricated without origin.

**Global-Local balance (55%)** — The craft is deeply Mongolian. The customers are worldwide. The work speaks universally because it speaks specifically. Local roots, global resonance.

**Quiet (90%)** — Almost silent. The brand whispers. The work speaks. Anything loud would be a lie about who we are.

**Slow (90%)** — Forty hours per boot. Thirty-five years of practice. Generations of technique. Nothing about Domog is fast. Speed is the enemy of craft.

**Soft-leaning (70%)** — Not sharp edges and hard contrasts. The softness of worn leather, of light through windows, of tools shaped by use. But not *too* soft—there's strength in the stitching.

#### What is uniquely Domog?

**The Nomadic Permanence.**

None of these traditions carry what Domog carries: the paradox of rootedness without settlement. The Mongolian craftsman's hands know leather because his ancestors knew horses, knew the steppe, knew that everything you own must survive movement.

This creates a unique aesthetic truth: *permanence without heaviness*. British heritage is anchored to estates. Italian craft to ateliers. Japanese reverence to tea rooms. 

Domog is anchored to *skill itself*. The workshop could move tomorrow; the hands would still know what they know.

This means: **warmth without coziness, permanence without weight, craft without preciousness.**

**Domog looks like** — Late afternoon light falling across a workbench where the same hands have worked for thirty-five years. Cream walls. Leather in all its life stages. Tools so used they've become beautiful.

**Domog sounds like** — The soft thump of a mallet. Thread being pulled through leather. Silence comfortable enough to work in. No background music. No urgency.

## PART 2: COLOR PHILOSOPHY

### Beyond Palette—Color Meaning

Color is not decoration. Color is memory. Every color in the Domog system must answer: *Where would I see this in the workshop?*

---

### Primary Colors — The Foundation

| | Color | Name | Hex | Meaning | Usage |
|---|-------|------|-----|---------|-------|
| ![](https://via.placeholder.com/30/0A0A0A/0A0A0A) | Charcoal | `charcoal-900` | `#0A0A0A` | The carbon of aged leather. The shadow under the workbench. Seriousness without severity. | Primary text. Dark sections. Grounding moments. The "voice" of the brand. |
| ![](https://via.placeholder.com/30/FAF8F3/FAF8F3) | Cream | `cream-50` | `#FAF8F3` | The natural color of aged paper. The light through workshop windows. Warmth without yellow. | Primary background. The "canvas" on which everything rests. The air in the room. |

#### Charcoal, not Black

Pure black (`#000000`) is digital. It belongs to screens, not workshops. It's the absence of light, not the presence of shadow.

Charcoal is what black becomes when you mix it with workshop dust. It's softer. It breathes. It doesn't fight the cream—it converses with it.

**Emotion:** Authority without aggression. Permanence. The weight of things that matter.
**Workshop origin:** The iron of tools. The shadow side of leather. The ink of old ledgers.
**Use:** Text, dark backgrounds, moments of gravity.
**Forbidden:** Never use for aggressive emphasis. Never at 100% opacity over images.

#### Cream, not White

Pure white (`#FFFFFF`) is surgical. Sterile. The color of things that have never been touched.

Cream is what white becomes when you add thirty-five years of sunlight. It's the page of a book that's been read. The wall that's absorbed thousands of hours of work.

**Emotion:** Warmth. Welcome. The feeling of being in a space where good work happens.
**Workshop origin:** Afternoon light. Unbleached canvas. The natural tone of unstained wood.
**Use:** Primary background. Breathing room. The space where products rest.
**Forbidden:** Never cold. Never stark. Never without a hint of warmth.

#### Gold — The Sacred Accent

Gold is not decoration. Gold is *emphasis*. It appears where brass would appear in the workshop: on tools that have been polished by use, on hardware that earns its place.

**Emotion:** Warmth, significance, the glow of craft done well.
**Workshop origin:** Brass fittings. The buckle on a finished boot. Tool edges worn to shine.
**Use:** Dividing lines that mark transitions. Hover states for primary actions. Moments of completion.
**Forbidden:** Never for body text. Never in large blocks. Never as attention-seeking decoration. The moment gold feels loud, it's wrong.

**The Gold Rule:** Gold must be earned. Before using gold, ask: "Is this moment significant enough?" If you're not sure, use cognac instead.

#### Cognac — The Working Accent

Cognac is the color of leather that's been worn for a season. It's gold's more practical sibling—warm but not precious, rich but not flashy.

**Emotion:** Warmth, naturalness, the comfort of materials that age well.
**Workshop origin:** Mid-toned leather. The stain on the workbench. Cardboard patterns aged by use.
**Use:** Category labels, secondary text, hover states, the everyday accent work.
**Forbidden:** Never cold. Never gray-toned. Must always read as "leather brown," not "fashion brown."

### Light & Dark Modes

#### Does Domog have a dark mode?

**Yes, but it's not a "mode"—it's a mood.**

Dark sections exist within the light experience (heritage sections, footer, certain immersive moments). But a full "dark mode toggle" would contradict the brand.

**Why?**
- The cream warmth is the workshop. It's the afternoon light. It's the feeling of the space.
- A full dark mode would change the brand's emotional temperature from "warm workshop" to "evening gallery."
- The dark sections work *because* they contrast with the light. They're chapter breaks, not alternate realities.

**The Dark Palette (for dark sections):**

| Element | Color | Note |
|---------|-------|------|
| Background | `charcoal-900` | Never pure black |
| Primary text | `cream-50` | The light text |
| Accent | `gold-600` | Glows more intensely against dark |
| Secondary | `stone-warm` | Subtle, readable |

**When Dark Appears:**
- Heritage/story sections (narrative chapter breaks)
- Footer (grounding, permanence)
- Immersive product galleries
- Never: Full-page dark. The light must return.

## PART 3: TYPOGRAPHY PHILOSOPHY

### Type as Voice

If Domog were a voice:

**It is a declaration made quietly.** Not a whisper—whispers are uncertain. Not a shout—shouts are desperate. It's the statement of someone who doesn't need to repeat themselves.

**It is the voice of an elder who doesn't demand respect—he simply has it.** He speaks because something is worth saying. He's silent because silence is also a statement.

**It speaks in short truths.** Not because it can't manage complexity, but because it's refined complexity into essence. "A machine can measure a boot. Only hands can know it."

**There is poetry in it, but not performance.** The rhythm emerges from meaning, not from trying to sound beautiful. The words land because they're precise, not because they're decorated.

---

### Typeface Selection

#### Serif — Headlines: Playfair Display

| Attribute | Specification | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Era** | Transitional, 18th-century inspired | Classical enough for heritage, refined enough for modernity |
| **Weight** | 400 (Regular), 500 (Medium), 600 (SemiBold), 700 (Bold) | Elegant range without heaviness |
| **Personality** | Editorial gravitas, confident, slightly romantic | Like a well-written book about a well-made thing |
| **Use** | Headlines, product names, pull quotes | Any text meant to be noticed first |

**Why Playfair Display?**
- High contrast strokes echo the contrast of leather craft (rough hide → refined boot)
- Slightly romantic without being decorative
- Reads as "editorial" not "corporate" or "tech"
- Beautiful at large sizes, where it will mostly live

**Typography Example:**
```
DISPLAY: "Legacy Carved by Hand"
         — Playfair Display, 600 weight, -0.02em tracking
```

---

#### Sans-Serif — Body: Inter

| Attribute | Specification | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Era** | Contemporary humanist | Modern clarity with warmth |
| **Readability** | Optimized for screens, excellent at body sizes | Long-form comfortable |
| **Weight** | 300 (Light), 400 (Regular), 500 (Medium), 600 (SemiBold) | Full range for hierarchy |
| **Personality** | Clear, honest, quietly confident | Like the craftsman's explanation—no wasted words |
| **Use** | Body text, descriptions, navigation | Anything meant to be read, not just seen |

**Why Inter?**
- Humanist warmth (not cold geometric sans)
- Exceptional legibility at body sizes
- Wide language support (critical for Mongolian)
- Neutral enough to let content lead

---

#### Utility — Labels, Navigation: Inter (modified treatment)

| Attribute | Specification | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Treatment** | Uppercase, tracked | Signals "functional" vs. "content" |
| **Tracking** | 0.15em – 0.25em | Adds air, signals "label" role |
| **Weight** | 400–500 | Visible but not dominant |
| **Size** | 10–12px (0.625–0.75rem) | Small but legible |
| **Role** | Categories, navigation, tags, CTAs | The scaffolding that guides without leading |

---

### Type Scale

Based on a **1.25 ratio** (Major Third) — warm, readable, not dramatic.

```

DISPLAY-LG   72px / 4.5rem    — Hero headlines
DISPLAY-MD   56px / 3.5rem    — Section titles
DISPLAY-SM   44px / 2.75rem   — Subsection titles
DISPLAY-XS   36px / 2.25rem   — Card headlines, product names

BODY-LG      18px / 1.125rem  — Pull quotes, lead paragraphs, emphasis
BODY-MD      16px / 1rem      — Standard reading, descriptions
BODY-SM      14px / 0.875rem  — Captions, secondary information
BODY-XS      12px / 0.75rem   — Fine print, legal

LABEL-LG     13px / 0.8125rem — Navigation, prominent labels
LABEL-MD     11px / 0.6875rem — Category labels, overlines
LABEL-SM     10px / 0.625rem  — Tags, smallest functional text
```

**When to use what:**

| Level | When |
|-------|------|
| Display-XL | Homepage hero only. The first word someone sees. |
| Display-LG | Page heroes, major section entrances. Once per page. |
| Display-MD | Section headlines within a page. 2-4 per page maximum. |
| Display-SM | Subsections, card titles, product names. |
| Display-XS | Supporting headlines, accordion titles. |
| Body-LG | Pull quotes. Lead paragraphs. Moments of emphasis. |
| Body-MD | Everything else. The workhorse. |
| Body-SM | Captions, metadata, supporting details. |
| Label-MD | The overline ("Est. 1990"). Categories. Navigation. |

### Typographic Details

#### Line Height

| Context | Line Height | Rationale |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| **Display (headlines)** | 1.1 – 1.2 | Tight. Headlines are visual. They should feel like blocks. |
| **Body (reading)** | 1.7 | Generous. Reading should feel unhurried. |
| **Labels** | 1.3 | Moderate. Functional but not cramped. |

#### Letter Spacing

| Context | Tracking | When |
|---------|----------|------|
| **Display headlines** | -0.02em | Large text needs tightening |
| **Body text** | 0 (normal) | Don't touch. Trust the typeface. |
| **Uppercase labels** | 0.15em – 0.25em | Adds air, signals "utility" |
| **Small text (<14px)** | 0.01em – 0.02em | Slight opening helps legibility |

#### Paragraph Spacing

```
Between paragraphs: 1.5em (one-and-a-half line heights)
After headlines: 0.75em
Before new sections: 3em minimum
```

#### Maximum Line Length

```
Body text: 65-75 characters (optimal)
Absolute maximum: 85 characters
Absolute minimum: 45 characters

Headlines: No limit, but should feel like single thoughts
```

#### Hyphenation

**No.** 

Hyphenation breaks the visual rhythm. It says "this text was poured into a container" rather than "this text was placed with intention." Short words on new lines are preferable to broken words.

#### Smart Quotes & Dashes

```
Always use:
- Smart quotes: "quoted text" not "quoted text"
- Apostrophes: craftsman's not craftsman's
- Em dashes: with no spaces — like this
- En dashes: for ranges (2020–2025)

Never use:
- Straight quotes: " or '
- Double hyphens: -- instead of —
- Ambiguous dashes
```

## PART 4: SPACE PHILOSOPHY

### The Luxury of Emptiness

> "Space is not absence. Space is the most expensive material."

In a world where attention is fragmented and screens are crowded, **restraint becomes the signal of luxury.** Anyone can fill a page. It takes confidence to leave it empty.

But there's a line:

**Luxury space** feels considered. Each element has room to breathe. You sense that someone chose what NOT to show.

**Empty space** feels uncertain. Like something is missing. Like the designer ran out of content.

The difference? **Proportion and rhythm.** Luxury space follows a system. Empty space is accidental.

---

### Macro Spacing — Section Rhythm

| Viewport | Section Padding | Breathing Room | Rationale |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|-----------|
| Mobile (<640px) | `py-16` (64px) | Generous for small screens | Avoid cramped feeling |
| Tablet (640-1024px) | `py-24` (96px) | Middle ground | Comfortable reading |
| Desktop (>1024px) | `py-32` to `py-40` (128-160px) | Maximum breathing | Luxury spacing |

**Does spacing increase with screen size?**

Yes. By approximately 1.5x from mobile to desktop.

**Why?** Larger screens afford more space. Using the same padding on desktop as mobile would feel cramped—like a large room with small furniture. Scale with the canvas.

---

### Micro Spacing — Element Relationships

| Relationship | Spacing | Token | Example |
|--------------|---------|-------|---------|
| Eyebrow → Headline | 16-24px | `space-4` to `space-6` | "Est. 1990" → "Legacy Carved by Hand" |
| Headline → Body | 24-32px | `space-6` to `space-8` | Title → First paragraph |
| Paragraph → Paragraph | 24px | `space-6` | Between text blocks |
| Label → Content | 8-12px | `space-2` to `space-3` | "Category:" → "Riding Boots" |
| Image → Caption | 12-16px | `space-3` to `space-4` | Photo → Description |
| Card padding | 32px | `space-8` | Internal breathing room |
| Grid gap (products) | 32-64px | `space-8` to `space-16` | Between product cards |
| Icon → Text | 8-12px | `space-2` to `space-3` | ✓ → "Free Shipping" |

---

### Spatial Rhythm — The Base Unit

**Base unit: 8px**

Everything derives from 8. This creates visual harmony through mathematical relationship.

```
TIGHT:      8px   (base × 1)    — Icon gaps, tight inline elements
DEFAULT:    16px  (base × 2)    — Standard component gaps
COMFORTABLE: 24px (base × 3)    — Paragraph spacing, small sections  
LOOSE:      32px  (base × 4)    — Card padding, medium sections
GENEROUS:   48px  (base × 6)    — Large section gaps
DRAMATIC:   64px+ (base × 8+)   — Section padding, hero spacing
```

**The Rule:** If a measurement isn't divisible by 8, question it. There may be a good reason—but it should be a *reason*, not an accident.

---

### Container Widths

```
MAX-CONTAINER:  1280px (80rem)   — Maximum content width
WIDE:           1120px (70rem)   — Standard page content
READABLE:       768px (48rem)    — Long-form text, optimal reading
NARROW:         640px (40rem)    — Focused content, forms
TIGHT:          480px (30rem)    — Modals, centered callouts
```

**Why 1280px maximum?**

Beyond this, the eye must travel too far. Lines become too long. Content feels untethered. 1280px is wide enough for generous product grids, narrow enough to feel curated.

## PART 5: IMAGERY PHILOSOPHY

### Photographic Identity — The Domog Lens

If there's one rule, it's this: **The photograph should feel like a memory of being in the workshop, not a manufactured image of a product.**

| Attribute | Domog Standard | Notes |
|-----------|----------------|-------|
| **Lighting** | Natural, directional, golden | Window light preferred. The light that shows leather's true color. No flash. No ring lights. |
| **Temperature** | Warm (5000-5500K) | Leather is warm. The image should feel warm. Never cool, never clinical. |
| **Contrast** | Medium-soft | Not flat, not dramatic. The gentle contrast of real spaces. |
| **Saturation** | Natural to slightly muted | Rich but not oversaturated. The colors of real materials, not retouched fantasy. |
| **Focus** | Sharp on subject, natural fall-off | Shallow depth of field acceptable for detail shots. Products should be fully sharp. |
| **Grain** | Subtle, natural | Clean is fine. Slight film grain is welcome. Adds warmth and tactility. |
| **Post-processing** | Minimal | Color correct, don't color grade. Don't make the leather look better than it is. |

---

| Subject | Purpose | Treatment |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Hands at work** | The core truth | Real hands, real work, mid-process |
| **Raw materials** | Authenticity | Leather hides, thread spools, brass hardware |
| **Process moments** | Transparency | Cutting, stitching, lasting, finishing |
| **Finished products** | The result | Clean, well-lit, respectful space |
| **Workshop environment** | Context | Tools, benches, light, atmosphere |
| **Details & textures** | Craft evidence | Stitching, grain, embossing, patina |
| **The wooden lasts** | Provenance | The forms that hold memory |
| **Mongolian landscape** | Heritage (subtle) | Horizon, steppe, sky—not tourist shots |

#### What Never Appears

| Subject | Why Forbidden |
|---------|---------------|
| **Stock photography** | Destroys authenticity instantly |
| **Over-styled lifestyle** | We're not selling a lifestyle, we're selling a boot |
| **Models as focus** | The boot is the hero, never the person |
| **Artificial studio setups** | If it couldn't exist in the workshop, it doesn't exist |
| **Celebrity endorsement imagery** | "We keep no photographs" is the point |
| **Trend-driven styling** | Nothing that will look dated in 2 years |
| **Posed "authenticity"** | If it's staged to look unstaged, viewers sense it |

---

### Composition Rules

#### Aspect Ratios

| Context | Ratio | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|
| **Hero images** | 16:9 or 21:9 | Cinematic, immersive |
| **Product (primary)** | 3:4 or 4:5 | Portrait orientation, room to breathe |
| **Product (grid)** | 1:1 or 4:5 | Consistent, orderly |
| **Detail shots** | 1:1 | Focused, intimate |
| **Environment** | 16:9 or 3:2 | Landscape, documentary |
| **Process/hands** | 4:3 or 3:2 | Workmanlike, honest |

#### Cropping Philosophy

**Product shots:** Never crop into the product. Leave generous space around it. The boot needs room to exist, not be crammed into a frame.

**Detail shots:** Tight is good. This is where you crop close—showing grain, stitching, texture. These are the "proof of craft."

**Environment:** Breathe. Show the space around the subject. Workshop shots should feel like being in a room, not looking at a wall.

#### Negative Space

**In product photography:** The background matters. A boot on cream felt, with generous margins, says "this object deserves space." A boot crammed into a frame says "we couldn't afford a bigger shot."

**Rule:** At least 15-20% of a product frame should be empty space.

#### Color Harmony

Photography should feel like it belongs in the Domog palette. This means:
- Warm tones preferred
- Cool tones avoided unless natural (metal tools, winter light)
- Background tones complementing cream/cognac palette
- No jarring color clashes

#### Text Overlay

**When acceptable:** 
- Hero images with proper overlay treatment
- Editorial/heritage sections

**When forbidden:**
- Product photos (let the product speak)
- Detail shots
- Process photography

**Treatment when used:**
- Always use gradient or solid overlay for readability
- Minimum 60% contrast between text and background
- Never obscure the important part of the image

---

### AI-Generated Imagery

**The Question:** Domog claims 35 years of human hands. Can AI-generated images ever be appropriate?

**The Answer:** *Contextually, with honesty.*

**Acceptable Uses:**
- Atmospheric backgrounds where no specific craft claim is made
- Conceptual/editorial imagery clearly not representing "our workshop"
- Placeholder during development (clearly marked internally)
- Steppe/landscape imagery where authenticity isn't claimed

**Never Acceptable:**
- Images claiming to show "our craftsman" or "our workshop"
- Product shots (must be real products)
- Process/hands-at-work imagery (the whole point is human hands)
- Anything that would feel like deception if discovered

**The Test:** If a customer discovered the image was AI-generated, would they feel deceived? If yes, don't use it.

**The Path Forward:** AI imagery is a bridge to real photography, not a destination. Every AI image should have a plan to be replaced with authentic documentation of the real workshop, real hands, real work.

## PART 6: MOTION PHILOSOPHY

### Movement as Craft

How does Domog move? The answer starts with understanding what movement means:

**Motion is a statement about time.** Fast motion says "we're busy, no time to waste." Slow motion says "watch this, it matters." No motion says "this is permanent, unchanging."

For a brand that spends 40 hours on a single boot, **motion should be deliberate, unhurried, meaningful.**

---

### Core Principles

**Motion is a tool, not a feature.** Every animation must have a purpose. "Because it looks cool" is not a purpose.

**The tempo is slow.** Nothing snaps into place. Things arrive, settle, become. Like a craftsman's hands—efficient but never rushed.

**Motion should feel like breathing.** In... out. Ease... release. Natural rhythm.

**Less is more.** When in doubt, don't animate. Stillness is appropriate for a brand about permanence.

---

### Animation Specifications

| Element | Motion Type | Duration | Easing | Notes |
|---------|-------------|----------|--------|-------|
| **Page transitions** | Fade | 300-400ms | `ease-in-out` | Subtle. Not cinematic. |
| **Hover states** | Color/opacity shift | 200-300ms | `ease-out` | Quick but not instant |
| **Scroll reveal** | Fade up (subtle) | 500-700ms | `ease-out` | Gentle arrival, not theatrical |
| **Menu open/close** | Slide + fade | 300-400ms | `ease-in-out` | Smooth, not bouncy |
| **Image gallery** | Crossfade | 400-500ms | `ease-in-out` | Respectful transitions |
| **Loading states** | Pulse or subtle spin | 1000-2000ms | `linear` | Calm, not anxious |
| **Accordion expand** | Height + fade | 300-400ms | `ease-out` | Natural reveal |
| **Toast notifications** | Slide in + fade | 300ms | `ease-out` | Noticeable but not aggressive |

**Easing Philosophy:**

```
ease-out    — Default. Things arrive and settle.
ease-in-out — For reversible actions (open/close)
ease-in     — Rarely. For exits if needed.
linear      — Only for continuous animations (loading)
bounce      — Never. Too playful.
elastic     — Never. Too trendy.
```

---

### The Golden Rule

> Motion should feel like **a door opening slowly, not a pop-up ad.**

If an animation makes you think "look at me," it's wrong.
If an animation makes you feel "something happened," it's right.

---

### What Never Moves

| Element | Why |
|---------|-----|
| **Navigation (core)** | Stability. The anchor must stay anchored. |
| **Primary product images** | The product is the truth. Truth doesn't wiggle. |
| **Prices** | Trust. Nothing should feel manipulative. |
| **Typography (body)** | Reading should be stable. |
| **Logo** | Identity. Never animated. |

## PART 7: TEXTURE & MATERIALITY

### Digital Tactility

The challenge: How do you make a screen feel like leather? The answer isn't texture *on* everything—it's texture *through* implication.

#### Textural Elements

| Texture | Where Applied | Purpose | Treatment |
|---------|---------------|---------|-----------|
| **Grain/noise** | Heritage sections, dark backgrounds | Warmth, organic feel | Very subtle (5-10% opacity) |
| **Shadows** | Cards, floating elements | Depth, physicality | Soft, warm-toned |
| **Borders** | Rare, intentional | Definition, craft detail | Fine (1px), subtle colors |
| **Gradients** | Overlays, depth | Atmosphere, transition | Extremely subtle |
| **Patterns** | Almost never | — | Only if directly from craft tradition |

---

### Shadow Philosophy

Shadows are how we create depth. They should feel like natural light, not effects.

## PART 8: SIGNATURE ELEMENTS

### What Makes Domog Instantly Recognizable?

Beyond logo. Beyond name. What says "Domog" with the logo hidden?

---

#### Signature 1: The Warmth

**The warm cream.** Not white. Not gray. That specific cream that feels like afternoon light in a workshop. (`#FAF8F3`)

**Why it works:** In a sea of white websites and dark modes, the warm cream is immediately distinctive. It signals "this is not a tech company" before anything else registers.

---

#### Signature 2: The Quiet Gold

**Gold used sparingly.** Dividers. Hover states. Moments of emphasis. Never decorative, always earned.

**Why it works:** Most luxury brands pour gold everywhere. Domog's restraint makes the gold feel precious. When you see it, you know it matters.

---

#### Signature 3: The Documentary Voice

**Short sentences. Specific numbers. No adjectives that don't earn their place.** "Forty hours. Wooden lasts. Hands that remember."

**Why it works:** The copy is so distinctive it becomes visual. You could recognize a Domog headline in a different typeface because the *rhythm* is unmistakable.

---

#### Signature 4: The Breathing Room

**Generous space around products.** No crowding. No maximizing. Every boot has room to exist.

**Why it works:** Space is confidence. Domog has the confidence to show less.

---

### The Details That Matter

| Detail | Domog Standard | Rationale |
|--------|----------------|-----------|
| **Border radius** | `0.25rem` (4px) — subtle softness | Not sharp, not round. The gentle wear of used tools. |
| **Shadow depth** | Soft, multi-layer, warm | Suggests natural light, not digital effect |
| **Divider style** | 1-2px, gold or cognac, generous space | Elegant, not structural |
| **Icon style** | Line icons, 1.5px stroke, rounded caps | Crafted, not clip-art |
| **Button shape** | Slightly rounded rectangle (`4px`) | Approachable but serious |
| **Input style** | Subtle border, warm focus state | Functional but considered |
| **Link treatment** | Underline on hover, cognac color | Respectful, not aggressive |

## PART 10: THE AESTHETIC TEST

### Validation Questions

Before any design decision, ask:

| Question | If "no"... |
|----------|-----------|
