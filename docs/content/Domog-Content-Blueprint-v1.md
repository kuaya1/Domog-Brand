# Domog — Website Content Blueprint

**Version:** 1.0 · October 20, 2026  
**Website:** https://www.domogbrand.com/en  
**Purpose:** Establish the content architecture, English direction, Mongolian editorial brief, and implementation handoff for the next version of Domog.  
**Status:** Working editorial blueprint. Sample copy is proposed, not approved for publication. Gemini has not yet written or translated this package, and Claude Code has not implemented it.

## 1. The direction

Present Domog through its Mongolian boots, the family behind the business, its history since 1990, and the actual work that goes into its products. Let customers understand the people and the product before asking them to order.

**Working positioning:** A Mongolian boot business with a family history beginning in 1990.

**Recommended public-facing direction:**

> Mongolian boots. A family story since 1990.

This is a starting point grounded in the owner's description and the site's product category. More specific language about handcraft, workshop location, materials, and production can follow once the family confirms it.

The desired feeling is warm, assured, precise, and personal. Premium perception should come from a clear product presentation, meaningful details, identifiable makers, and dependable service information.

## 4. Audience and context

Do not assume the international audience is the family's main revenue source simply because the inspected URL is English.

| Customer situation | What the visitor needs | Content response |
|---|---|---|
| Mongolian customer choosing boots for an occasion | Familiar terminology, suitable styles, sizing, availability, local ordering details | Natural Mongolian copy, use-based guidance, clear contact/order path |
| Mongolian customer living abroad | Cultural connection plus confidence in fit, payment, shipping, and timing | Family story, measurements, transparent international process |
| International customer unfamiliar with Mongolian boots | Orientation to the form, use, meaning, materials, and styling | Short explanations, real-life images, plain product information |
| Returning or referred customer | Find a known pair or contact the family quickly | Search, recognizable names, concise navigation, easy contact |

These are working audience hypotheses, not verified customer demographics. Ask the family which situations account for most actual purchases.

The English and Mongolian versions should share the same facts. They may explain those facts differently because customers bring different cultural knowledge.

## 5. Voice and language rules

### The voice

- Use a collective family voice for ordinary brand copy. Identify the speaker whenever a passage uses a personal first-person memory.
- Be welcoming and self-assured. Give people useful information without requiring them to demonstrate cultural knowledge or taste.
- Prefer concrete verbs: cut, shape, stitch, fit, inspect, wear, care, repair. Use each only where the process is confirmed.
- Give each paragraph a job. An emotional opening can lead into a factual explanation.
- Let a distinctive detail carry the story. A real tool, remembered first order, or specific design decision can support a paragraph.
- Use Mongolian terms where they add meaning; explain them briefly for English readers.
- Acknowledge machine-assisted work accurately when relevant. Describe the actual division of work without implying that machinery makes craft less valuable.

### Language to reconsider

| Current tendency | New editorial direction |
|---|---|
| Frequent claims of exceptional luxury | Explain the material, construction, finish, and service |
| Repeated references to sacredness or ancient lineage | State the precise cultural meaning and its source where known |
| Broad references to prominent wearers | Use documented, permission-appropriate examples or omit |
| European luxury and watchmaking comparisons | Explain the family's own standards and influences; include outside influences only if they belong to its real history |
| Absolute claims about lifespan or immediate comfort | Explain expected use, fit, care, and product-specific limits |
| Poetic language in transaction controls | Use recognizable action labels that explain what happens next |
| Repetition of the same craft promise across every section | Give each section fresh information |

### Proposed UI vocabulary

| Purpose | English direction | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Main navigation | Collection · Our Family · Visit & Contact | Preserve routes; use short mobile labels |
| Product card | View details | Takes the customer to a product page |
| Direct purchase | Add to bag | Use only if that is what the control does |
| Inquiry-led order | Ask about this pair | Sends or opens an actual inquiry path |
| Real custom commission | Request a custom pair | Use only when the service is confirmed |
| Sizing | Size & fit | Same terminology on product and support pages |
| Aftercare | Care & repair | Separate instructions from paid/covered services |
| Order action | Review order / Continue to payment / Place order | Match the actual step and consequence |

Do not change a working transaction action to an inquiry, or vice versa, merely to match this table. Confirm the business model first, then label the real action clearly.

## 8. Homepage blueprint and English starting copy

The copy below is a directional draft. Bracketed fields are internal requirements and must never appear on the public site.

### H01 — Introduction

**Job:** Identify the product, family, and history immediately.

- Eyebrow: `DOMOG · A FAMILY BUSINESS SINCE 1990`
- H1: `Mongolian boots. A family story since 1990.`
- Body: `Our family began this business in 1990. Explore our collection and get to know the people behind Domog.`
- Primary CTA: `Explore the collection`
- Secondary CTA: `Meet our family`
- Image: An actual current pair. Its caption and alt text should describe what is visibly shown.

After process confirmation, decide whether an explicit handmade claim belongs in this opening. Do not assume every model shares the same production method.

### H02 — The collection

**Job:** Let visitors move from the brand introduction to an appropriate pair.

- Heading: `Find your pair`
- Body direction: One sentence about the available range, using only confirmed occasions and product types.
- Show a concise selection of real products. Use actual names, current prices/currency, availability, and one useful distinguishing fact per pair.
- Card CTA: `View details`
- Section CTA: `View the collection`

Do not introduce new categories or rename products before the family confirms the catalog. Explain differences customers can use: lining, height, construction, intended occasion, fit, or weather suitability.

### H03 — The family

**Job:** Make the people behind the boots identifiable.

- Heading: `The family behind Domog`
- Safe starting line: `Our family’s work began in 1990. This website is a place to share our boots and the story behind the business.`
- Final paragraph structure: `[Parent names] + [how the business began] + [each person's actual contribution] + [what they do today]`.
- Image: A named family portrait or genuine workshop photograph with a factual caption.
- CTA: `Read our story`

Keep the mother's and father's contributions accurate and visible. Do not invent equal roles, manufacturing tasks, or present-day involvement to create symmetry.

### H04 — How the work is done

**Job:** Give the craft language a visible basis.

- Heading: `A closer look at the work`
- Three modules: `[confirmed material choice]`, `[confirmed making step]`, `[confirmed finishing/inspection step]`.
- Each module: 35–55 words, an actual image if available, and one observable detail.
- Example pattern: `For [model], [maker] uses [material] because [practical reason]. [Specific step] gives the boot [supported result].`

If process facts or documentary images are unavailable, keep this module out of the publishable draft and record the missing input. Do not fill it with generic workshop imagery presented as the family's own work.

### H05 — Boots in use

**Job:** Connect the product to real life and current tradition.

- Heading direction: `Worn for [confirmed occasions]` or `Domog, worn today`.
- Show actual use in a ceremony, celebration, performance, daily setting, or riding context only where applicable to the featured pair.
- Captions explain the pair, occasion, and relevant detail. Obtain permission for identifiable customers.
- Use educational cultural notes only after family/Gemini review.

This section can begin with one well-documented example. It does not require a large gallery.

### H06 — Choosing and ordering

**Job:** Explain what the customer can expect next.

- Heading: `Choosing your pair`
- Step 1: `Explore the collection` — compare the styles and details.
- Step 2: `Check your fit` — use the approved guide or ask the family for help through a real channel.
- Step 3: `[Actual order step]` — direct checkout, inquiry, or custom-order process, according to confirmed operations.
- Include one plain sentence distinguishing available stock, making time, and shipping where relevant.
- CTA: `Size & fit` or the verified contact action.

### H07 — Care and continued use

**Job:** Explain the relationship after the sale.

- Heading: `Looking after your boots`
- Safe starting line: `Read our care guidance, or contact us with a question about your pair.`
- Add repair/service details only after confirmation. State paid services and defect coverage separately.
- CTA: `Read the care guide`

### H08 — Contact and footer

- Contact invitation: `Questions about a pair? Get in touch.`
- Footer descriptor direction: `Mongolian boots · Family business since 1990`.
- Verified business contact details and visit instructions.
- Support links: Size & Fit; Shipping & Returns; Care; Warranty.
- Newsletter only if there is a real program, working subscription, and an agreed reason to subscribe. Describe actual content/frequency; do not promise an invented schedule or offer.
- Recommendation: defer the discount-popup decision until the family confirms its promotional policy. Any change to that behavior is a separate implementation decision, not an automatic copy edit.

## 9. Our Family page

### Narrative structure

1. **A personal introduction:** Names, relationship, current roles, an actual portrait.
2. **The beginning in 1990:** Place, first work, motivation, and circumstances in the family's own account.
3. **Each parent's contribution:** The work each did at different stages; include management, customer relationships, design, sourcing, and making where applicable.
4. **What developed over time:** A small number of verified milestones. Distinguish the business's beginning from the brand name, workshop move, or website launch.
5. **How Domog works today:** Who does what now, which processes remain, which have changed, and why.
6. **What they want to carry forward:** One approved quotation or clearly attributed paraphrase.
7. **An invitation:** View the collection or contact the family.

### Draft opening

> Our family began this business in 1990. Domog brings that history together with the boots we offer today. Here, we want to introduce the people behind the business and share how their work has developed over the years.

Replace this general opening with a more personal passage when family answers arrive. Do not keep generic introductory text simply because it is easy to translate.

### Editorial constraints

- Write memories from recorded answers, notes, or identifiable family sources.
- Use direct quotation only for words the speaker actually said and approved.
- Do not invent childhood scenes, economic hardship, apprenticeships, titles, famous customers, or generational counts.
- Explain cultural tradition separately from the documented history of this business.
- Keep dates precise where known; use honest approximations when the family remembers only a period.
- An initial target is 500–800 words, adjusted to the actual material. A shorter factual story is acceptable.

## 11. Fit, delivery, care, and contact

### Size & Fit

Use one owner-approved measurement system across the standalone page, product modal, calculator, product options, and order record. Clearly identify whether a table refers to men's, women's, unisex, or another manufacturer-specific sizing system. Do not infer this from a generic internet conversion.

Explain foot measurement, intended socks, foot width and calf measurement where relevant, differences by model, and how to ask for help. Describe custom fitting only if it is offered. Do not automatically advise sizing up or down for every model.

### Production and delivery

Use this information structure, populated with confirmed values:

| Stage | Customer-visible information |
|---|---|
| Availability | In stock / made after order / custom |
| Making or preparation | Estimated time before dispatch, or actual stock dispatch range |
| Shipping | Service, destination-dependent transit estimate, tracking if offered |
| Charges | Shipping, taxes/duties handling, personalization or other applicable fees |
| Total expectation | Estimated overall arrival window and relevant dependencies |

Express transit must not imply express production. Avoid one worldwide delivery promise where destination or service differs. Show the relevant summary at the product/order decision as well as the full support page.

### Returns and warranty

Reconcile existing statements with the actual policy before revising public promises. Specify differences among stock orders, standard made-after-order products, personalized products, and fully custom products where the business treats them differently.

Separate workmanship coverage from wear, maintenance, and paid repair. State eligibility, process, charges, transport responsibility, and limits in both languages. Do not invent a lifetime promise, free exchanges, prepaid international labels, or a response guarantee. Preserve records of prior terms and existing customer obligations.

### Care

Organize by actual leather/finish/lining needs. Confirm instructions with the maker or appropriate material supplier. Check that recommended products are suitable for the relevant finish. Describe realistic water/weather limitations. Use short instructions that a customer can follow while holding the boots.

### Contact

Proposed title: `Visit & contact`.

Proposed introduction: `Questions about a pair, sizing, or an order? Contact us using the details below.`

Include exact visit instructions if in-person visits are available, a map/directions link if accurate, working contact channels, local time zone for hours, and appointment requirements if applicable. Do not publish a response-time promise until the family can support it.

Keep the form concise. Include model/size context when a real product inquiry flow supports it. Describe a form submission as successful only after the backend confirms receipt. When a feature is not connected, give a real alternative instead of a simulated success message.

## 12. Gemini's Mongolian writing and translation brief

### The two-way process

1. ChatGPT provides structure, English intent, sample copy, and the fact ledger.
2. The family provides history and technical language in whichever language is easiest, including Mongolian notes or voice transcripts.
3. Gemini extracts factual statements from those materials, distinguishing direct quotations from paraphrases.
4. Gemini writes natural Mongolian content within the architecture and drafts/refines corresponding English.
5. Gemini supplies a literal English meaning-check for culturally important or materially changed passages.
6. ChatGPT reviews meaning, evidence, positioning, completeness, and customer-flow consistency.
7. The family checks personal history, names, terminology, cultural meaning, and operating promises.
8. Claude receives the reconciled content package with publishable strings clearly separated from internal questions.

Translation can improve the English source. When Mongolian expresses the idea more accurately, Gemini should propose a change to the source intent rather than forcing an awkward equivalent.

### Language requirements

- Working assumption: natural modern Mongolian in Cyrillic for the existing Mongolian audience. Confirm against the actual locale and family preference before finalizing. Do not introduce vertical-script Mongolian or new locale routes without a separate decision.
- Use the family's terminology for boot types, components, techniques, and occasions after checking it for customer comprehension.
- Avoid literal translations of English luxury idioms.
- Preserve names and product identity. Record approved spellings in Mongolian and Latin characters as needed.
- Do not invent a meaning for Domog or a model name. Ask for confirmation before publishing an etymology.
- Avoid treating one workshop's practice as universal across Mongolian culture.
- Keep product specifications, dates, amounts, eligibility, and policy scope equivalent across languages.
- Never translate units or currencies by silently changing the underlying values.
- Flag uncertainty visibly in the working package. Do not use a fluent translation to conceal a missing fact.

### Required glossary

|---|---|
| Brand and model names | Approved Cyrillic/Latin spellings, meaning if verified, capitalization |
| Boot types and parts | Family term, customer-friendly Mongolian term, precise English equivalent |
